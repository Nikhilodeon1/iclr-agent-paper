\subsection{Validation on a Pretrained Model and Natural Text}
\label{sec:realtext}

The experiments above train from scratch on a synthetic corpus, so the trigger's clean
frequency is set by construction. To test the same prediction where that frequency is a
property of real text, we continue pretraining Pythia-70m \citep{biderman2023pythia} on
WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017pointer}, selecting triggers by their \emph{measured} corpus
frequency (Appendix~\ref{app:realtext} gives the full protocol).

\textbf{Result.} Theory predicts that the poison needed scales with the number of
competing clean uses the model sees during training, $f \cdot T$ for a trigger of
per-step frequency $f$, and does not grow with $T$ when $f=0$. We ran four frequency levels, 5 seeds per cell and $T=600$ steps, with the ordering and a slope range specified before running. The interpolated poison count for 50\% attack success is $9.1$ for the zero-frequency trigger (bootstrap 95\% interval over seeds $[8.7,9.6]$) and $33.4$ ($[33.1,33.8]$), $35.7$ ($[33.4,39.5]$) and $80.0$ ($[62.3,107.7]$) for triggers occurring $0.050$, $0.200$ and $0.500$ times per step, against predicted $30.1$, $120.1$ and $300.1$ competing clean uses. The thresholds increase with measured corpus frequency, as Proposition~\ref{prop:fraction-regime} predicts, and the zero-frequency trigger is the cheapest to implant, by a factor of $3.7$ to $8.8$. The ordering is clearest at the extremes: at $N=64$ mean attack success is $0.95$, $0.93$, $0.86$ and $0.44$ across the four levels, and at $N=16$ it is $0.84$ for the zero-frequency trigger against $0.01$ or less for the other three. The two middle levels are close: their thresholds differ by $7\%$ and the full ordering holds in $98\%$ of bootstrap resamples of seeds, but these intervals are conditional on a fixed grid (the interval at $f=0.050$ is narrow because nearly every seed lies near $0.93$ at $N=64$), and at $N=64$ the per-seed ranges overlap ($0.91$--$0.96$ against $0.68$--$0.98$; two-sided Mann--Whitney $p=0.22$). We treat the order of those two levels as suggestive.

The proportionality prediction fails, as we expected before running it. The thresholds are $1.11\times$, $0.30\times$ and $0.27\times$ the predicted number of competing clean uses, and the log-log slope of threshold against predicted uses over the three nonzero levels is $0.35$ (bootstrap interval $[0.25,0.47]$), against the range $0.7$--$1.3$ we accepted. Over this range the threshold therefore grows with frequency \emph{sublinearly}. We report this rather than resolve it; the scalar model gives us no means to predict it. Each nonzero estimate is interpolated across a fourfold bracket of the grid ($N=16$ to $64$, or $64$ to $256$ at the highest frequency), so the agreement at $f=0.050$ is not evidence of quantitative accuracy. This experiment supports the ordering only. Clean validation loss lies between $4.36$ and $4.66$ across all runs; no unpoisoned continuation was run as a baseline, so we cannot say whether poisoning changes clean loss, we did not test for a trend with poison count, and we draw no conclusion about detectability from it.

\textbf{Scope.} The model is pretrained rather than trained from scratch, the corpus is natural text, and trigger frequencies are measured rather than imposed, so the $\rho$ transition is tested outside the setting that produced it. It is a small model on a small corpus, and frequency is measured on WikiText-2 rather than on the corpus the model was pretrained on; we do not reproduce the pretraining-scale behaviour of \citet{souly2025poisoning} and do not claim to.
