\subsection{Validation on a Pretrained Model and Natural Text}
\label{sec:realtext}

The experiments above train from scratch on a synthetic corpus, so the trigger's clean
frequency is set by construction. To test the same prediction where that frequency is a
property of real text, we continue pretraining Pythia-70m \citep{biderman2023pythia} on
WikiText-2 \citep{merity2017pointer}, selecting triggers by their \emph{measured} corpus
frequency (Appendix~\ref{app:realtext} gives the full protocol).

\textbf{Result.} Theory predicts that the poison needed scales with the number of
competing clean uses the model sees during training, $f \cdot T$ for a trigger of
per-step frequency $f$, and does not grow with $T$ when $f=0$. Three of four frequency levels completed in time for this
version. Over 2 seeds per cell and $T=600$ steps, the interpolated poison count for 50\% attack success is $8.8$ for the zero-frequency trigger and $30.7$ and
$43.9$ for triggers occurring $0.050$ and $0.200$ times per step, against predicted
$30.1$ and $120.1$ competing clean uses. The thresholds are ordered as Proposition~\ref{prop:fraction-regime} predicts: they rise monotonically with measured corpus frequency, and the rare trigger is the cheapest to implant by a factor of $3.5$--$5$. We caution against reading more into the numbers. Each nonzero-frequency estimate is interpolated between only the $N=16$ and $N=64$ grid points, a fourfold bracket, from 2 seeds; the $1.02\times$ agreement at $f=0.050$ is therefore not evidence of quantitative accuracy, and at $f=0.200$ the threshold is $0.37\times$ the linear prediction, so over this range it grows with frequency \emph{sublinearly}. We report that discrepancy rather than resolve it, and the scalar model gives us no means to predict it. This experiment supports the ordering only. Clean validation loss stays within $0.18$
across every run ($4.35$ to $4.53$) with no trend in poison count, but no unpoisoned continuation was run as a baseline, so we cannot say whether poisoning changes clean loss at all, and we draw no conclusion about detectability from it.
